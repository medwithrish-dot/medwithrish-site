# Interview stations that still need product input

This note records the station types that are not yet specified enough to build confidently. It is intentionally separate from the question bank: the questions should remain original, but they need to follow the format and assessment rules agreed here.

## Already clear enough to develop

- **Ethical dilemma:** a balanced discussion, with answer-aware probes where useful. The interviewer should not interrupt a well-structured answer simply to force a probe. Marking should reward an open-minded consideration of competing views and penalise a one-sided answer or a shallow interviewer/candidate ping-pong.
- **Scenario:** a detailed, original scenario with several facts that produce practical knock-on effects. The candidate should identify the people affected, notice the details, explore alternative outcomes and set out a structured response.
- **Skills:** questions about teamwork, conflict, leadership or another skill. A strong answer uses the logic of STARR conversationally, gives a specific example and ends with genuine reflection.
- **Role-play with a standardised actor:** a candidate handles a difficult conversation, followed by “What went well?” and “What could have gone better?”. Bad-news tasks can use SPIKES as a flexible structure. The candidate should communicate concisely, respond to emotion with empathy rather than detached sympathy, and notice actor/interviewer cues.

## Input still needed

### Group station

Please define:

- whether this is a real multi-candidate exercise, an AI-simulated group or an individual question about group behaviour;
- typical tasks, group size and whether candidates receive written material;
- whether the outcome matters, or whether assessors mainly observe listening, inclusion, leadership and collaboration;
- how the platform should capture contributions from other group members;
- the main traps and any behaviours that should never be rewarded (for example, domination disguised as leadership).

### Communication station

Please define:

- whether the candidate speaks to an actor, the AI interviewer or a written character;
- the intended task types: breaking down information, responding to distress, handling anger, persuasion, explaining risk, or another format;
- what information the candidate is and is not allowed to know;
- whether empathy, information gathering, checking understanding and agreeing next steps are the core marking areas;
- when escalation, safeguarding or professional boundaries should become relevant.

This should remain distinct from the standardised-actor role-play unless the intended product design is to merge both under one station type.

### Role-play content still required

The format and marking approach are now clear, but each station still needs an original actor pack containing:

- the candidate brief and actor brief;
- facts the actor may disclose only if asked;
- emotional state, likely reactions and cues;
- safe boundaries for what the applicant is expected to explain;
- a branch guide so the actor responds consistently without forcing one exact script;
- a station-specific checklist and the intended next step.

Please also confirm whether applicants should ever simulate a doctor breaking a terminal diagnosis. This can test communication, but the brief must avoid requiring clinical authority or detailed cancer counselling beyond an applicant's level. A non-clinical bad-news task, such as explaining that a pet has died, can assess the same communication skills with fewer clinical assumptions.

### Prioritisation station

Please define:

- the usual number and type of competing tasks;
- whether the candidate may ask for missing information;
- whether new information should arrive part-way through the station;
- the expected prioritisation principles: immediate safety, urgency, impact, delegation, deadlines and reassessment;
- whether a single final ranking is required or whether a justified flexible plan is preferred.

### Background to medicine

Please clarify the distinction between **general** and **not general** background questions. In particular:

- which areas belong here: the NHS, the role of a doctor, training, multidisciplinary teams, public health, medical careers or university-specific course knowledge;
- whether current NHS problems and solutions should sit here or under hot topics;
- how current and source-dependent the expected knowledge should be;
- whether university-specific stations should use only verified information from official admissions/course pages.

### Data interpretation

Images are deliberately deferred. Before implementation, please provide or approve:

- the visual types to support: charts, tables, infographics, study abstracts or combinations;
- the expected level of arithmetic and whether calculators are allowed;
- accessible text alternatives for every image;
- whether the station should reveal information progressively;
- the preferred traps: confusing percentage with percentage points, ignoring denominators, claiming causation, missing uncertainty or overlooking bias;
- original or licensed source material and the intended answer for each visual.

## Cross-station decisions

The following decisions would also improve consistency:

- preparation time, if any, for each station type;
- whether “about one question per 2.5 minutes” has station-specific exceptions;
- which stations may generate answer-aware follow-ups and the maximum number;
- whether unanswered prompts count as incomplete or are simply assessed from the evidence already given;
- whether station titles shown after entry should remain generic or reveal the exact topic.
